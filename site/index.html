No more dead zones while you browse

Your phone can usually tell a dropout is coming, because the signal fades first. Shajarah uses that warning to switch networks early and download what you'll read next, so a tunnel, a lift or a satellite gap doesn't interrupt you.

The problem

Phones hop between Wi-Fi, 5G and satellite all the time. Every switch can break open connections, and a real outage (a road tunnel, say) leaves you staring at a spinner. Existing fixes either keep the connection alive but have nothing to show you offline, or download pages ahead of time all the time and waste data.

Websites
  1. See it coming. The device watches signal strength. When it's fading, it predicts a dropout a few seconds ahead.
  2. Switch early. The tunnel opens a connection on the next network before the current one dies, so nothing breaks.
  3. Stock up. The server guesses which links you'll click next and sends those pages ahead of time. It sends more when a dropout is near and less when things are fine.
  4. Read offline. During the gap, pages open instantly from the device's cache.

What's new here

Connection migration (QUIC) and link prefetching both exist already. What we add is linking them: how aggressively we prefetch depends on how likely you are to lose your connection, and a language model predicts which links you'll click.
